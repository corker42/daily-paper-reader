# Proof-Grounded Patient-Specific Clinical Explanations from Knowledge-Graph Reasoning 

Surajit Das mr.surajitdas@gmail.com 

Abstract —Clinical decision-support outputs can lack an au-ditable link between patient observations, encoded knowledge, conclusions, and recommendations. We present the CKG Clin-ical Explanation Engine, a downstream layer for a frozen, training-free clinical knowledge-graph reasoner that converts patient inference states and disease knowledge into typed facts, explicit rule-application traces, provenance-linked conclusions, and policy-licensed recommendations. The design separates mea-surement availability, representation completeness, and disease-specific activation; consequently, observed zero-activation evi-dence is not treated as missing and partial representation is distinct from unobserved evidence. Optional language genera-tion is restricted to symbolically licensed content. Across five usable workbooks (6,720 patients; 20,160 patient–disease traces; 1,021,440 feature-evidence rows), IG-range validity and knowl-edge provenance were 100%, numerical cross-sheet fidelity was 100% (120,960/120,960), and exported logical/report trace com-pleteness was 100% (20,160/20,160). Availability–representation consistency was 99.7028% (1,018,404/1,021,440); all 3,036 dis-agreements were confined to three systematic feature–cohort patterns. The corpus contained 86,783 observed zero-activation and 139,949 observed partially represented instances. A separate seeded 25-patient end-to-end audit completed without execution failure and passed all pre-specified trace, licensing, provenance, and state-consistency checks. These results establish structural and implementation auditability, not clinical correctness or util-ity. 

Index Terms —clinical knowledge graph, symbolic reasoning, explainability, provenance, auditability, clinical decision support 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Clinical decision support increasingly combines statistical, knowledge-based, and language technologies, but a clinically readable explanation is not necessarily an auditable one. A useful explanation should expose how patient observations, encoded domain knowledge, intermediate conclusions, and permitted actions are connected. Post-hoc explanations can describe model behavior, while large language models (LLMs) can improve presentation, but neither property alone guaran-tees that every reported claim or recommendation is licensed by the underlying reasoning state [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]. This work introduces the CKG Clinical Explanation En-gine , a downstream explanation layer for the training-free, ontology-grounded clinical knowledge-graph (CKG) reasoner developed by Das [1]. The upstream diagnostic computation is frozen: the explanation engine neither retrains nor recal-ibrates it and does not alter diagnostic scores or mappings. Instead, it converts existing patient–disease inference outputs and disease knowledge into typed facts, rule-application traces with explicit premises, provenance-linked conclusions, and recommendations constrained by a closed policy. The contribution is threefold. First, the engine separates measurement availability, representation completeness, and disease-specific activation, preventing a zero activation value from being interpreted as missing data. Second, it produces inspectable symbolic traces linking facts to conclusions and licensed actions. Third, we evaluate the frozen implementation at corpus scale and through an end-to-end engine audit. Optional language realization occurs only after symbolic li-censing and cannot independently introduce diagnoses, mea-surements, tests, medications, or recommendations. Extended algorithms, audit tables, traces, and implementation details are provided in the supplementary material (https://tinyurl.com/ tijarus). II. R ELATED WORK AND POSITIONING 

Explainability in high-stakes settings is often discussed in terms of post-hoc accounts of model behavior [2], [3]. Such explanations can be useful, but they are different from an execution record showing which encoded facts and rules licensed a particular conclusion. Clinical LLM studies like-wise demonstrate substantial capability while documenting limitations in factuality, calibration, and decision-making [4], [5], [6]. These observations motivate a constrained role for language generation rather than its use as the authority that determines clinical content. Knowledge-graph-based clinical decision-support research has also used explicit semantic structure to improve trans-parency and expose relationships underlying recommenda-tions [7]. More generally, provenance models such as W3C PROV provide a standardized vocabulary for representing how entities and activities contribute to derived information [8]. The present work addresses a narrower implementation problem: preserving an auditable patient-specific path from frozen upstream reasoning outputs through distinct availability, representation, and activation states to explicit rule applica-tions and policy-licensed actions. The present work is therefore positioned as a downstream symbolic audit layer . It does not propose another diagnostic classifier and does not compare predictive discrimination with outcome-fitted models. The diagnostic mapping is inherited unchanged from the upstream CKG reasoner of Das [1]. The contribution examined here is whether a patient-specific rea-soning state can be transformed into an inspectable explanation in which observations, representation status, disease-specific arXiv:2610.06549v1 [cs.AI] 5 Oct 2026 TABLE I FROZEN UPSTREAM INTERFACE USED BY THE EXPLANATION ENGINE .

III. M ETHOD 

A. Relationship to the Frozen CKG Reasoner 

For patient p, disease D, and feature f , the upstream system of Das [1] exports patient values, feature mappings, clinical roles, disease-level scores, evidence coverage, and a disease-specific information-gate value IG D (p, f ) ∈ [0 , 1] . The ex-planation layer treats these quantities as immutable facts. Disease-level scores are therefore evidence to be explained, not quantities recomputed by the explanation engine. The processing chain is CKG output + knowledge/policy → typed facts → rule traces 

→ licensed claims/actions → report .

The boundary between the two systems is intentional. The upstream reasoner owns disease scoring and evidence activation; the explanation engine owns only interpretation and trace construction. Table I summarizes the interface used by the explanation layer. A change to the explanation renderer therefore cannot feed back into the diagnostic score. 

B. Orthogonal Evidence State 

The original implementation contains information about whether a measurement exists, how completely it is repre-sented by the encoded components, and whether it activates disease-specific evidence. These concepts are kept separate. Let mpf ∈ {0, 1} denote measurement availability and 

apf ∈ [0 , 1] representation coverage. Then 

Observed( p, f ) ⇔ mpf = 1 , (1) 

Unobserved( p, f ) ⇔ mpf = 0 , (2) 

Unrepresented( p, f ) ⇔ mpf = 1 ∧ apf = 0 , (3) 

Partial( p, f ) ⇔ mpf = 1 ∧ 0 < a pf < 1, (4) 

Full( p, f ) ⇔ mpf = 1 ∧ apf = 1 . (5) The activation IG D (p, f ) is disease-specific and is not an availability flag. In particular, IG D (p, f ) = 0̸ ⇒

Unobserved( p, f ). Thus an observed measurement may con-tribute zero disease-specific activation, and an observed feature may simultaneously be partially represented. This formulation removes the ambiguity created by treating “observed,” “par-tial,” and “unobserved” as competing states.

Here “proof” denotes an inspectable rule-application certifi-cate: each exported step records its premises, rule identifier, conclusion, and reason/provenance. It does not denote inde-pendent proof-assistant verification of logical soundness or clinical truth. Recommendations are emitted only when the symbolic state and the explicit recommendation policy jointly license them. Low coverage, partial representation, or an LLM cannot independently create a test recommendation. A deterministic renderer is the evaluated reporting path. An optional LLM may realize already licensed content in natural language, subject to checks that numerical values originate from facts and actions belong to the policy; medication and dose generation are disabled. The LLM path is not evaluated here. This creates a closed recommendation boundary. For ex-ample, an observed feature with IG D (p, f ) = 0 can be de-scribed as observed but non-activating; it cannot be relabelled “missing.” Likewise, partial representation can be surfaced as an uncertainty in encoded coverage, but it cannot itself authorize a test. A test action requires both an unresolved genuinely unobserved feature and a matching policy entry, as in (9). Consequently, the explanatory state may expose uncertainty without automatically converting that uncertainty into an intervention. Each rule application is exported with four audit-relevant fields: premises, rule identifier, conclusion, and rea-son/provenance. Trace completeness in this paper means that these required fields are present for the exported rule ap-plication. It does not imply that an external theorem prover has certified the rule base. This operational definition is used consistently in the evaluation below. 

D. Trace Construction and Audit Contract 

For each patient–disease pair, the explanation engine con-structs the trace from already exported feature-level evidence rather than reconstructing the upstream diagnostic calculation. A patient value is first associated with its mapped feature and disease-specific role. Availability is then read independently from representation coverage and activation. This ordering is important: an IG value is interpreted only after the system knows whether the underlying measurement was observed, and coverage is interpreted as a property of representation rather than acquisition. The resulting typed facts form the premises consumed by the explanation rules. A trace step is considered structurally complete only when the exported artifact contains the premises consumed by the rule, a rule identifier, the resulting conclusion, and a human-readable reason or provenance reference. The deterministic report is generated from these trace-level conclusions rather than directly from raw workbook cells. Thus, the report can be audited backwards from a sentence or action to a conclusion, from the conclusion to a rule application, and from the rule application to patient and knowledge facts. This is the operational sense in which the explanation is “proof-grounded.” The recommendation policy is deliberately narrower than the descriptive trace. The trace may describe observed sup-port, observed non-activation, partial representation, or un-resolved evidence, but only the final category can partici-pate in a confirmatory-test rule, and then only when a dis-ease/feature/test mapping exists in the packaged policy. This separates describing uncertainty from authorizing an action .It also provides a concrete verification target: every emitted recommendation must resolve to a policy identifier and every cited knowledge feature must resolve to packaged YAML provenance. IV. E VALUATION 

The evaluation used the frozen package and a separate reproducibility workflow without modifying upstream diag-nostic inference. Five non-empty CKG output workbooks were usable; an additional zero-byte influenza workbook was ex-cluded before analysis. Together they contained 6,720 patients, three disease traces per patient (20,160 traces), and 1,021,440 feature-evidence rows. The full-corpus audit checked: (i) availability/representation consistency; (ii) IG ∈ [0 , 1] ; (iii) coverage/component consis-tency; (iv) resolution of knowledge provenance to packaged YAML; (v) numerical agreement across exported sheets; and (vi) completeness of exported logical and deterministic-report traces. These are structural implementation invariants, not tests of clinical validity. A second, seeded end-to-end audit (seed 20260926) invoked the current patient reasoner for five patients sampled indepen-dently within each usable workbook ( n = 25 ); workbook-specific seeded sampling makes the selected identifiers re-producible across reruns. It checked successful execution, required proof-step fields, recommendation identifiers against the closed policy, YAML-source resolution, and consistency between availability and representation states. The audit re-tained exceptions rather than silently dropping failed cases. Full workbook-level results, patient identifiers, traces, runtime data, and failure files are placed in the supplementary material (https://tinyurl.com/tijarus). The two audit levels answer different questions. The corpus audit tests exported structures at scale, whereas the end-to-end audit executes the current explanation path and checks the resulting report artifacts. Keeping these levels separate avoids treating successful parsing of historical exports as equivalent to successful execution of the current engine. No outcome label is used by the explanation layer to create or validate a recommendation. 

A. Audit Independence, Regression Checks, and Statistical Interpretation 

The reproducibility workflow is implemented separately from the patient-report engine and reads the frozen work-books and packaged YAML artifacts directly. This separation reduces dependence on the report renderer when checking workbook invariants, numerical cross-sheet agreement, and provenance resolution. It is nevertheless not an independent formal verifier: the workflow and engine necessarily share the documented semantics of fields such as Observed , coverage, and policy identifiers. Accordingly, the reported pass rates are described as structural consistency and implementation checks, not independent certification of logical or clinical correctness. As an additional software-regression check, the supplied frozen package was executed against its focused patient-report, evidence-state, and FOL/HOL reasoner tests; all six selected tests passed. These tests exercise report construction and the evidence-state logic but are not counted as patient-level validation cases. Deliberate mutation testing of proof objects was not part of the frozen experiment and is therefore not claimed here; adversarial mutation of premises, rule identifiers, provenance, and policy identifiers remains a useful future validation of validator sensitivity. The corpus percentages are descriptive proportions over exported rows. Feature rows are nested within patients and workbooks and therefore should not be treated as independent Bernoulli trials. For this reason, no binomial confidence inter-val is attached to the 99.7028% consistency rate in the revised analysis. The seeded end-to-end audit likewise reports exact pass counts rather than estimating a population-level clinical error rate. We additionally retained two naturally occurring stress conditions rather than filtering them: observed features with 

IG = 0 and observed features with 0 < a pf < 1. These cases directly test the state semantics because collapsing activation, representation, and availability would misclassify them. The failure table was also retained in full so that systematic disagreements could be localized by workbook, disease, and feature instead of being hidden behind an aggregate pass rate. V. R ESULTS

D ISCUSSION 

The evaluation supports three implementation-level conclu-sions. First, measurement availability cannot be inferred from disease-specific activation: tens of thousands of observed in-stances legitimately had zero activation. Second, partial repre-sentation is a property of how an observed feature is encoded, not a synonym for missing measurement. Third, provenance and numerical fidelity can be checked mechanically across the exported reasoning artifacts. The systematic 3,036 disagreements are also informative. The earlier binary audit conflated an observed measurement with a representation record labelled missing . The revised formulation exposes the disagreement instead of forcing the two dimensions into one state. Because the current engine ob-tains availability from Feature_Evidence.Observed ,these rows do not by themselves become unobserved evidence; nevertheless, the legacy export disagreement should be cor-rected at its source and remains visible in the supplementary failure table. These results also clarify what the explanation layer con-tributes beyond a formatted report. A conventional renderer can reproduce values, but it need not preserve the distinction between a missing observation, an incompletely represented observation, and a present observation that contributes no disease-specific activation. Here those distinctions are explicit predicates and are therefore available to both the deterministic report and the reproducibility checks. This is particularly important in the 86,783 zero-activation and 139,949 partial-representation instances, where a single generic “missing/weak evidence” label would discard materially different computa-tional states. The recommendation boundary provides a second form of auditability. Recommendations are not inferred from narrative plausibility; they are consequences of a symbolic unresolved-evidence state plus a closed policy lookup. This makes the provenance of an action inspectable and prevents the optional language layer from expanding the action space. The design is intentionally conservative: a clinically sensible action absent from the policy is not silently invented, but instead requires an explicit knowledge/policy update outside the patient-level explanation run. The corpus and end-to-end audits should also be interpreted together. Corpus-scale checks provide breadth over more than one million feature-evidence rows, while the seeded engine audit verifies that the current executable pathway can construct complete trace and report artifacts for sampled patients. The former is stronger for detecting systematic export inconsis-tencies; the latter is stronger for detecting execution, policy-resolution, or report-construction failures. Neither substitutes for clinical validation. The claims are deliberately bounded. Passing a rule-trace or provenance check does not establish that a medical rule, threshold, knowledge entry, or recommendation policy is clin-ically correct. The study is cross-sectional and does not eval-uate prognosis, patient outcomes, clinician decision quality, or prospective clinical utility. The 25-patient engine audit is smaller than the corpus-level exported-structure audit and should not be interpreted as exhaustive end-to-end clinical validation. Likewise, no claim is made that the constrained architecture outperforms unrestricted LLM reporting; such a comparison addresses a separate empirical question. Clinical review, prospective validation, and broader deployment testing remain necessary. 

A. Validity Boundaries 

There are three distinct validity layers. Structural valid-ity asks whether exported values, provenance, trace fields, and policy identifiers are internally consistent; this is the principal target of the present experiments. Semantic validity 

asks whether the encoded medical roles, thresholds, and rules faithfully represent the intended clinical knowledge. Clinical validity asks whether using the resulting explanation improves decisions or patient outcomes. Passing the first layer does not establish the latter two. This distinction is important because a perfectly traceable system can still expose an incorrect encoded rule; traceability makes that error inspectable rather than clinically correct. The current evaluation also inherits the scope of the frozen artifacts. It is cross-sectional, uses the available disease knowl-edge and policy files, and evaluates the deterministic explana-tion path. The optional LLM realization layer is intentionally excluded from the reported pass rates. The end-to-end sample is reproducibly seeded but smaller than the corpus audit; its role is to exercise the executable pathway, not to estimate a clinical error rate. These boundaries motivate future clinician review of rule/policy content and prospective evaluation with-out changing the implementation-level conclusions tested here. 

B. Reproducibility Details 

The audit notebook fixes random seed 20260926 and records the selected patient identifiers, per-case status, proof-step count, recommendation count, and execution time. Excep-tions are written to dedicated failure files rather than removed from denominators. The corpus audit likewise writes every failed invariant to a row-level failure table containing work-book, patient/disease/feature context, test name, and diagnostic detail. This design permits aggregate numbers in the paper to be traced back to the underlying exported record. The zero-byte influenza workbook is excluded before analysis and is not included in any denominator. 

C. Reproducibility Materials 

To keep the conference paper within the conference page limit, the Reproducibility Materials contain the workbook-level structural-check tables, the complete 3,036-row fail-ure localization, sampled patient identifiers, machine-readable proof traces, runtime records, YAML knowledge/policy files, and experiment scripts/notebooks. The main paper reports only quantities that are directly supported by those frozen artifacts. The Reproducibility Materials are intended to permit inspec-tion of individual failures and trace steps without changing the scope of the claims made here (https://tinyurl.com/tijarus). VII. C ONCLUSION 

We presented a downstream explanation engine that leaves a training-free CKG diagnostic reasoner unchanged while con-verting its patient-specific state into typed facts, explicit rule-application traces, provenance-linked conclusions, and policy-licensed recommendations. Across 6,720 patients and 20,160 patient–disease traces, the implementation showed complete IG-range, provenance, numerical-fidelity, and exported-trace checks, while a 99.7028% availability–representation consis-tency audit localized all remaining disagreements to three systematic export patterns. Separating availability, represen-tation completeness, and disease-specific activation prevents observed zero-activation or partial evidence from being misre-ported as absent.
