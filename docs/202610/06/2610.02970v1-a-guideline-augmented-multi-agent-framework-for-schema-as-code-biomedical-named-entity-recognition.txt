Abstract —Large language models (LLMs) have shown promis-ing potential for biomedical named entity recognition (BioNER) through instruction following and in-context learning. However, existing LLM-based BioNER methods still face two key limi-tations. First, retrieved demonstrations and external biomedical knowledge provide limited support for dataset-specific annotation semantics, leaving entity boundaries, type scopes, and annota-tion conventions ambiguous. Second, free-form generation lacks sufficient structural control, often leading to invalid formats, hallucinated mentions, duplicated entities, and boundary errors. To address these limitations, we propose GAMA, a guideline-augmented multi-agent framework for schema-as-code BioNER. GAMA first induces candidate annotation rules from labeled training instances and verifies them against annotated data to construct reliable dataset-specific guideline memory. Guided by these verified rules, a planning component generates ranked span-type hypotheses with rationales, and a coding component converts them into schema-constrained entity objects. A verifi-cation module then checks span grounding, type validity, and structural compliance, and performs dual-loop refinement to correct invalid or low-confidence predictions. Experiments on five widely used BioNER datasets with multiple LLM backbones show that GAMA consistently outperforms strong LLM-based baselines. Ablation and parameter analyses further verify the effectiveness of the proposed components.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Biomedical named entity recognition (BioNER) aims to identify domain-specific entity mentions, such as diseases, chemicals, genes, proteins, and anatomical entities [1]. As a core task in biomedical information extraction, BioNER supports many downstream applications, including biomedical relation extraction [2] and biomedical event extraction [3]. Supervised neural models and domain-specific pretrained lan-guage models such as BioBERT [4] and PubMedBERT [5] have achieved substantial progress. Nevertheless, these ap-proaches require large-scale manually annotated corpora, which are costly due to the need for both linguistic expertise and domain knowledge. Large language models (LLMs) offer a promising alterna-tive, as they can perform downstream tasks using instructions and a few in-context examples without additional training [6]. Recent studies have mainly explored LLM-based BioNER from two perspectives: (i) retrieving similar annotated ex-amples as in-context demonstrations to provide instance-level evidence [7], [8], and (ii) incorporating external biomedical knowledge, such as entity semantics or UMLS information, to improve domain understanding [9], [10]. Despite their effectiveness, these approaches still face two major limitations. First, they provide limited support for dataset-specific anno-tation semantics. Retrieved examples usually reflect local an-notation decisions, while external knowledge mainly provides general biomedical information. As a result, dataset-specific entity boundaries, type scopes, and annotation conventions remain ambiguous, especially when entity definitions and annotation standards vary across datasets. Second, structural control remains insufficient. Since BioNER requires each pre-dicted entity to be grounded in the input sentence, assigned to a predefined type, and returned in a valid format, free-form LLM generation may produce invalid outputs, duplicated entities, hallucinated mentions, or boundary errors, which ordinary prompting cannot fully prevent. To address these limitations, we present GAMA, aguideline-augmented multi-agent framework for schema-as-code BioNER. Rather than prompting a single LLM to directly produce entity predictions, GAMA decomposes the extraction process into five specialized components: Annotation Rule In-duction, Rule Verification, Planning, Coding, and Verification with Dual-Loop Refinement, each responsible for a distinct stage of the task. GAMA is built upon three core principles: (1) Explicit Guideline Modeling , where the Annotation Rule Induction Agent summarizes dataset-specific annotation rules from training instances, and the Rule Verification Agent filters unreliable or overly instance-specific rules to construct explicit and reusable guideline memory; (2) Multi-Agent Decompo-sition , in which the Planning Agent generates mention-type hypotheses with explanatory rationales, ranks them according to their consistency with the input sentence, the predefined label schema, and the verified guideline rules, and the Coding Agent converts the most reliable hypotheses into schema-arXiv:2610.02970v1 [cs.CL] 2 Oct 2026 constrained code-style entity objects; (3) Schema-as-Code Verification , where each predicted entity object is validated for span grounding, type correctness, and structural validity, while a dual-loop refinement procedure corrects local object-level errors or revisits lower-confidence span–type hypotheses when necessary. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

We introduce a guideline modeling mechanism that in-duces and verifies dataset-specific annotation rules, pro-viding explicit dataset-level guidance for entity bound-aries, type scopes, and annotation conventions.  

> •

We propose GAMA, a guideline-augmented multi-agent framework that decomposes BioNER into rule induc-tion, rule verification, guideline-guided planning, schema-constrained coding, and prediction verification.  

> •

We develop a schema-as-code verification mechanism with dual-loop refinement, which checks span grounding, type validity, and structural compliance to reduce invalid, hallucinated, and inconsistent predictions.  

> •

We conduct experiments on five widely used BioNER datasets with multiple LLM backbones. The results show that GAMA consistently outperforms strong LLM-based baselines, and ablation studies further verify the effec-tiveness of its key components. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. BioNER with In-Context Learning 

BioNER remains challenging due to domain-specific termi-nology, complex entity boundaries, and annotation ambiguity. Traditional supervised approaches often require large-scale annotated corpora, which are expensive and time-consuming to construct in the biomedical domain. To reduce such an-notation dependence, recent studies have explored in-context learning (ICL) with LLMs, where models perform entity extraction by conditioning on natural language instructions and a few contextual examples without updating model parameters. Early work focused on improving the content of prompts to enhance LLMs’ understanding of the task. For instance, Hu et al. [11] designed a clinical NER prompt framework by incorporating annotation guidelines, error-analysis-based instructions, and few-shot examples, while Ge et al. [7] further investigated structured static prompts that integrate dataset descriptions, high-frequency entities, UMLS knowledge, and error feedback, demonstrating that careful prompt construc-tion can substantially improve few-shot BioNER performance. Beyond prompt content, other studies aimed to strengthen LLMs’ domain awareness and reasoning capabilities. Bian et al. [9] reformulated BioNER as a two-step process of span extraction followed by entity type prediction, augmented with UMLS-based external knowledge to support accurate type determination. Similarly, Li et al. [12] introduced the RT framework, which leverages retrieved demonstrations com-bined with Chain-of-Thought reasoning, guiding LLMs to gradually recognize entities with higher accuracy. These works highlight that effective BioNER often requires domain-specific knowledge and reasoning beyond simple text generation. More recently, retrieval-augmented demonstration selection has been explored to improve example selection in ICL. EICL [8] introduces an entity-aware retrieval strategy that combines semantic similarity with domain-specific terminology overlap to select more relevant in-context examples for BioNER. 

B. Multi-Agent Systems in Information Extraction 

Recent advances in large language models have promoted the use of multi-agent collaboration for information extraction, where different agents are assigned specialized roles to im-prove structured prediction through cooperation, refinement, and verification. Wang et al. [13] proposed CMAS, which employs a self-annotator, a type-related feature extractor, a demonstration discriminator, and an overall predictor to construct and filter self-annotated demonstrations for zero-shot named entity recognition. Lu et al. [14] introduced CROSSAGENTIE, a cross-type and cross-task multi-agent framework for zero-shot information extraction, where multi-ple agents collaborate to improve structured prediction across named entity recognition and relation extraction. Zhang et al. [15] developed a generation-evaluation multi-agent frame-work, where a generation agent synthesizes event data and an evaluation agent assesses semantic consistency to provide reward signals for iterative optimization. These studies indicate that role specialization, agent collaboration, and verification-oriented refinement are effective for improving the reliability of LLM-based structured extraction. III. M ETHODS 

We propose GAMA, a guideline-augmented multi-agent framework for schema-as-code BioNER. GAMA reformulates entity extraction as a schema-constrained programming pro-cess with explicit guideline modeling and iterative verification. The framework consists of five components: (1) an Annotation Rule Induction Agent that induces dataset-specific annotation rules from training data, (2) a Rule Verification Agent that validates and filters induced rules, (3) a Planning Agent that generates ranked mention-type hypotheses, (4) a Coding Agent that converts these hypotheses into executable entity objects, and (5) a Verification and Dual-Loop Refinement module that performs prediction-level validation and iterative correction. E. Verification and Dual-Loop Refinement 

After the Coding Agent generates schema-constrained entity objects, GAMA applies a prediction-level verification and refinement mechanism to ensure that the outputs are both text-grounded and schema-compliant. Each generated object is checked before being included in the final prediction set. Given the input sentence x, the dataset-specific schema S(s),and the generated entity objects C = {ci}Ki=1 , each object ci is verified along three dimensions: span grounding, type validity, and structural compliance. Span grounding checks whether the predicted mention appears in x, type validity checks whether the predicted type belongs to T (s), and structural compliance checks whether the object follows the schema S(s). Formally, this prediction-level verification is defined as: 

(Vi, ϵ i) = Vpred (ci, x, S(s)), (8) where Vi indicates whether ci passes the verification checks, and ϵi denotes diagnostic feedback used for refinement. To improve robustness, GAMA employs a dual-loop refine-ment mechanism. The outer loop follows the ranked hypothe-ses produced by the Planning Agent and prioritizes candidates with higher βi. The inner loop uses the diagnostic feedback ϵi

to revise and re-check the corresponding entity object until it passes verification or reaches the maximum number of refinement attempts. Only verified objects are included in the final prediction set: 

Y(s)(x) = {(mi, t i) | Vi = 1 , c i = EntityObject( mi, t i)}.

B. Baselines 

We compare our method with four representative LLM-based NER baselines, all of which perform inference without fine-tuning LLMs on task-specific data.  

> •

GPT-NER [21] transforms NER into a text generation task by asking LLMs to mark entity spans with special tokens, followed by self-verification to mitigate halluci-nated predictions.  

> •

CodeIE [22] converts information extraction as code generation, using Code-LLMs to generate structured pre-dictions in Python-style lists and dictionaries under few-shot settings.  

> •

CMAS [13] performs zero-shot NER with a cooperative multi-agent framework that integrates self-annotation, type-related feature extraction, demonstration discrimina-tion, and final prediction.  

> •

EICL [8] selects demonstrations for BioNER through entity-aware in-context learning, combining sentence-level semantic similarity, entity-centric representations, and domain-specific vocabulary overlap. 

C. Base LLMs 

We evaluate our method and all baselines on top of multiple representative instruction-tuned LLMs to ensure a compre-hensive comparison across model families, parameter scales, and closed-source/open-source settings. The evaluated models include Llama3-8B-Instruct and Llama3-70B-Instruct from the Llama3 family [23], Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct from the Qwen2.5 family [24], and two proprietary OpenAI models, GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4o [25]. 

D. Implementation Details 

Our framework is built upon instruction-tuned LLM back-bones without task-specific fine-tuning. For fair comparison, the same annotated training split is used whenever labeled information is required: baselines select in-context examples from it, while GAMA uses it only to induce and verify dataset-specific guideline memory. All components are driven by natural-language prompts and communicate through structured intermediate outputs, enabling multi-agent collaboration with-out updating model parameters. Unless otherwise specified, the same LLM backbone is used for all components. For guideline memory construction, the top-n verified annotation rules are retained for each entity type, where n = 10 . The Planning Agent generates k = 3 ranked candidate hypotheses, and the maximum number of inner-loop refinement iterations is set to t = 3 . All experiments use greedy decoding with 

temperature = 0 . We report the average results over three independent runs. 

E. Evaluation Metrics 

Following previous work [8], we adopt the Micro-F1 score as the primary evaluation metric for all methods. We use exact-match evaluation, where an extracted entity is counted as correct only if its span boundary and entity type are both identical to the gold annotation. Micro-F1 provides an entity-level measure that balances precision and recall over all entity categories, enabling fair comparison with existing NER methods. V. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS

B. Generalization across LLMs 

The results in Table III demonstrate that the proposed method exhibits strong cross-model generalization ability across both open-source and proprietary LLMs. Specifically, our framework consistently ranks first on all datasets and all backbone models, verifying that the performance improve-ments are not tied to a specific LLM family. From the perspective of backbone scaling, a clear positive correlation can be observed between model capability and final BioNER performance. As the backbone evolves from Qwen2.5-14B to Qwen2.5-72B and further to GPT-4o, nearly all methods show performance improvements; however, our method benefits the most from stronger LLM reasoning capacity. For instance, on NCBI, the proposed framework improves from 75.12 under Qwen2.5-14B to 78.91 under Qwen2.5-72B and further to 79.71 under GPT-4o. Similar trends are consistently observed on BC2GM and AnatEM. Compared with traditional prompt-based baselines such as GPT-NER and CMAS, the proposed framework achieves substantially larger margins, suggesting that structured code-oriented reasoning is more effective than conventional natural-language prompting for biomedical en-tity extraction. Moreover, even compared with the strongest baseline EICL, our approach still achieves stable gains across nearly all datasets and backbone models, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed collaborative reasoning and verification mechanism. Notably, the improvements become more pronounced on datasets involving complex biomedical terminology and long-span entities, such as BC4CHEMD and AnatEM. This indicates that the proposed framework can better capture structured semantic dependencies and reduce boundary ambiguity through iterative multi-agent refinement. C. Ablation Study 

Table IV evaluates five key design choices of GAMA across two LLM backbones and three BioNER datasets. All ablated variants consistently underperform the full model, with similar trends observed for Llama3-70B and GPT-4o, demonstrating the robustness of the proposed design. Removing the entire guideline modeling pathway, including Rule Induction and Rule Verification, decreases F1 by 3.53–4.89 points across the evaluated settings. This confirms the importance of incorporat-ing dataset-specific annotation knowledge into the extraction process. When only Rule Verification is removed, perfor-mance decreases by 2.79–3.96 points. Moreover, the gains of using unverified rules over using no guideline modeling are relatively limited and dataset-dependent. These results indicate that rule induction alone may introduce noisy or overly instance-specific guidance, while verification is impor-tant for constructing a reliable guideline memory. Removing Planning Rationales produces the largest average degradation among the evaluated variants, with F1 reductions ranging from 2.96 to 6.09 points. This demonstrates that explicitly relating candidate mentions and entity types to the induced annotation guidelines helps organize the prediction process and improves boundary and type decisions. Notably, this variant retains candidate generation and removes only the rationale-based reasoning mechanism. Removing Schema-as-Code results in consistent reductions of 1.45–2.87 F1 points, showing that schema-constrained representations provide addi-tional benefits over unconstrained entity generation. Disabling the Verification Loop causes larger drops of 2.20–4.15 points, particularly on BC4CHEMD under both backbones. This finding is consistent with the role of iterative verification and correction in identifying inconsistent predictions and improv-ing the reliability of complex biomedical entity extraction. Overall, the results demonstrate that GAMA benefits from the complementary interaction of verified guideline modeling, rationale-guided planning, schema-constrained representation, and iterative refinement.

E. Impact of k and t

To further evaluate the proposed iterative reasoning frame-work, we analyze two key hyperparameters: the number of generated hypotheses k and the maximum refinement itera-tions t. Table VI summarizes the results on three biomedical NER datasets. Several observations can be made. First, in-creasing k improves performance in most settings, indicating that multiple candidate hypotheses help the model explore diverse semantic interpretations of biomedical entities. This is particularly beneficial for datasets with complex entity structures and ambiguous terminology, such as BC4CHEMD. Second, increasing t also leads to noticeable gains, demonstrat-ing the effectiveness of iterative refinement and self-correction. Additional refinement iterations allow the framework to repair inconsistent predictions and improve entity boundary accuracy. Nevertheless, the gains gradually saturate when both k and t

become excessively large. Although (k = 5 , t = 5) achieves competitive performance, it does not consistently outperform 

(k = 3, t = 3) . This suggests that overly large hypoth-esis pools may introduce noisy low-confidence candidates, while excessive refinement iterations can produce redundant corrections and reduce prediction stability. VI. C ONCLUSIONS 

In this paper, we presented GAMA, a guideline-augmented multi-agent framework for schema-as-code BioNER. GAMA addresses two key limitations of existing LLM-based BioNER methods: the lack of explicit modeling for dataset-specific an-notation semantics and the weak structural control of free-form generation. By inducing and verifying annotation guidelines from training instances, decomposing extraction into planning and coding stages, and validating schema-as-code entity ob-jects through dual-loop refinement, GAMA produces more grounded and structurally valid biomedical entity predictions. Experiments on five public BioNER datasets and multiple LLM backbones show that GAMA consistently outperforms strong LLM-based baselines. Ablation and parameter analyses further verify the effectiveness of each key component. These results highlight the importance of combining dataset-level annotation guidance with explicit structural constraints for reliable LLM-based BioNER.
