Abstract 

While medical multimodal large language mod-els (Med-MLLMs) advance medical visual question answering (VQA), existing clinical workflow–inspired multi-agent frameworks suf-fer from interaction patterns and excessive com-putational overhead caused by redundant com-munication topologies. In this paper, we pro-pose MedPrune , an efficient MED ical multi-modal multi-agent collaboration framework that dynamically PRUNE s both nodes and edges from the communication topology to en-hance reasoning ability and token efficiency. Specifically, we first formulate the diagnos-tic process as a heterogeneous communica-tion graph, where nodes represent specialist agents from various departments and edges capture intra- and inter-departmental interac-tions. Building on this graph, we introduce two sparsification mechanisms to enable adap-tive collaborative evolution: (1) Heterogeneous Node Sparsification, which eliminates task-irrelevant specialist agents irrelevant to the cur-rent multimodal question via reinforcement learning–driven topological optimization, and (2) Heterogeneous Edge Sparsification, which selectively retains only the most diagnostically salient intra- and inter-departmental connec-tions by jointly optimizing task performance and topological complexity. Extensive medical VQA experiments under full-set and few-shot training settings prove MedPrune surpasses multi-agent baselines and boosts token effi-ciency with strong adversarial robustness. 1

A single LLM-based model cannot capture the deep expertise of fields, and its performance deteriorates on complex cases requiring cross-department rea-soning (Zhu et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026). Previous works for medical VQA can be broadly categorized into three paradigms: (1) 

Training-free : single-agent prompting methods such as specialized chain-of-thought (CoT) (Wei et al., 2022) and RAG (Xia et al., 2024b; Wang et al., 2025a; Wu et al., 2026b), aim to improve rea-soning without model parameters updates. While these methods avoid costly fine-tuning and can incorporate external knowledge, they remain sus-ceptible to medical hallucinations when the do-main knowledge is insufficient (Zhu et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025). (2) Training-intensive : these methods are including supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning techniques, directly opti-mize model parameters on medical data (Elmekki et al., 2025; Aydın et al., 2026). While they achieve stronger modality alignment and improved factual grounding within a unified representation space, they require substantial volumes of high-quality an-notated medical data and expensive computational resource (Dong et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2026). (3) Multi-agent Collaboration : these works have recently emerged as a flexible paradigm that circumvents the need for model fine-tuning (Chen et al., 2025a; Du et al., 2026). By orchestrating multiple MLLMs-based agents to mimic clinical multi-departmental collaboration, they can gather different expert opinions to reach a consensus diag-nosis (Zhu et al., 2025). Fixed-Template Evolving relies on static and predefined communication templates, which leads to degraded diagnostic performance due to noisy redundant discussions. Adaptive Evolving can also yield a smaller communication subgraph in the final stage, yet its iterative topology refinement still incur substantial token overhead, failing to achieve true medical efficiency gains. In contrast, our MedPrune dynamically prunes irrelevant medical agents and redundant edges simultaneously, enabling token-efficient multi-department diagnostic reasoning (Rounds T2 ≪ T1). 

Such rigidity fails to capture the dynamic, case-adaptive nature of real-world clinical consultations, while token consumption escalates rapidly as more agents and discussion rounds are involved (Wu et al., 2026a; Yoon et al., 2026). Concurrently, several adaptive topology optimization methods have emerged to mitigate such overhead by either pruning or generating redundant agents or commu-nication edges based on states via learnable graph structures (Shao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026), yet their effectiveness in medical mul-timodal reasoning remains unexplored; see Fig. 1. In this paper, we introduce a MED ical multi-modal multi-agent PRUNING framework, named 

MedPrune , that formalizes medical diagnosis as a dynamic evolutionary graph learning over heteroge-neous multi-departmental collaboration. Our main contributions are summarized below: • Heterogeneous Node Sparsification: Ex-isting multi-agent medical systems typically communicate with all departments agents, thereby compelling models to allocate compu-tational resources to clinically irrelevant spe-cialties (Xia et al., 2025a; Du et al., 2026). To address this, we propose a heterogeneous node sparsification that selectively prunes diagnos-tically irrelevant specialist nodes by learning task-adaptive inter-departmental communica-tion graph. Specifically, we parameterize the heterogeneous graph with learnable soft ad-jacency matrices and optimize them via rein-forcement learning using task performance as the reward signal. Then, pruning nodes with minimal weighted degree along with their in-cident edges to dynamically assemble a com-pact team of relevant specialists and eliminate token overhead from the initial round. • Heterogeneous Edge Sparsification: Even after pruning irrelevant agents, redundant communication edges may persist, wasting tokens and propagating hallucinations across departments (Zhang et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2025). Hence, after re-initializing edge weights on the node-sparsified subgraph, we jointly optimize them under a composite ob-jective that maximizes task performance while penalizing topological complexity. This pro-cess prunes low-utility edges and retains only the most salient intra- and inter-departmental connections, ensuring that each remaining edge contributes high diagnostic utility. Two-stage sparsification fundamentally addresses efficiency bottlenecks in multi-agent medical reasoning, achieving high diagnostic perfor-mance with drastically reduced token costs. We evaluate MedPrune on three authoritative medical VQA benchmarks: MIMIC-CXR (John-son et al., 2019), IU-Xray (Demner-Fushman et al., 2016) and OmniMedVQA (Hu et al., 2024) under full-set and few-shot training settings. Our method consistently outperforms strong baselines across training-free, training-intensive, and medical multi-agent systems. Under both training settings, our ap-proach achieves an average performance improve-ment of 3.65% and a 37.86% boost in token effi-ciency. In addition, our method only reduces the average performance by 3.1% under the adversarial attack settings, which greatly proves the robustness of our system. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Medical Visual Question Answering 

Existing approaches can be primarily divided into two paradigms: (1) Training-free Methods. They aim to elicit better reasoning from frozen Med-MLLMs backbones. CoT (Wei et al., 2022) decom-poses complex clinical questions into intermedi-ate reasoning steps. Multimodal RAG (Xia et al., 2024b, 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Wu et al., 2026b) further supplies external knowledge such as medi-cal literature or electronic health records to ground the model’s response. (2) Training-intensive Methods. They focus on updating model param-eters on domain-specific corpora. SFT (He et al., 2024, 2025; Gong et al., 2025) aligns Med-MLLMs with medical image–text pairs, enhancing modal-ity fusion and factual grounding. Reinforcement learning works (Su et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026) further steers generation toward clinician-preferred outputs by optimizing for human-defined reward signals. While these techniques yield stronger in-distribution results, they demand large-scale and high-quality annotated medical data (Mota et al., 2024; Matos et al., 2025). 

2.2 Multimodal Multi-Agent Collaboration 

Research in this area falls into two categories: (1) 

Static Collaboration. AdaCoMed (Chen et al., 2025a) uses a mixture-of-modality-experts to fuse uni-modal representations and introduces adaptive co-learning coupling experts with a compact model. MedAgent-Pro (Wang et al., 2025c) operationalizes diagnostic protocols by decomposing clinical rea-soning into evidence-grounded stages. (2) Adap-tive Evolving Collaboration. Rising VQA com-plexity exposes limitations of static multi-agent systems, driving dynamic multi-modal collabo-ration (Ranaldi et al., 2025; Nie et al., 2026). MMedAgent-RL (Xia et al., 2025a) uses RL to learn optimal policies among medical agents. M3Prune (Shao et al., 2025) is a hierarchical com-munication graph pruning framework that elim-inates redundant edges. CARE (Du et al., 2026) decouples grounding and reasoning via coordinator-guided framework with explicit segmentation ev-idence. ARG-Designer (Li et al., 2026) reframes topology design as autoregressive graph generation. However, these methods primarily focus on generic collaboration, lacking specialized designs for het-erogeneous medical knowledge. In this paper, we jointly sparsify nodes and edges over the hetero-geneous graph, achieving more efficient evolution rather than surface-level adaptations.

4.1 Main Results 

We evaluate MedPrune on three medical multi-modal QA benchmarks covering various body parts (i.e., Chest, Bone, Eyes, and Thyroid) under full-set and few-shot training settings, against four types of strong baselines with Qwen3-VL (8B). As shown in Table 1, we can observe that: (1) Methodologically, the performance hi-erarchy reveals a clear progression in reason-ing capability. Training-free prompting (SP, CoT) and training-intensive single-agent methods (RAG, LoRA, GRPO) both suffer from limited cross-disciplinary reasoning capacity, with the for-mer being particularly vulnerable to hallucina-tions (Kim et al., 2025). Fixed-template systems incur heavy token overhead from static interac-tion patterns, while adaptive methods achieve only moderate topology refinement and remain sub-optimal for medical reasoning. Our MedPrune jointly prunes irrelevant specialist nodes and re-dundant edges, dynamically tailoring the graph per question, and consistently outperforms all baselines with superior accuracy and token effi-ciency. (2) Regardless of backbone generality (general-domain vs. domain-adapted) or scale (7B to 397B), MedPrune consistently achieves supe-rior performance. This robustness stems from its task-driven graph optimization, which is indepen-dent of specialized MLLM knowledge, while larger backbones universally improve all methods with more pronounced gains for multi-agent systems, owing to their enhanced multimodal alignment and instruction-following capabilities (Luo et al., 2025). (3) Regarding training strategy, few-shot training with only 10 samples enables MedPrune to achieve performance comparable to full-set training, par-ticularly with domain-adapted backbones. This stability is attributed to our task-driven graph opti-mization, which involves no MLLMs’ parameter updates and thus avoids the overfitting and repre-sentation disruption that plague training-intensive methods under extreme data scarcity (Zhu et al., 2026). Consequently, our model reduces data de-pendency, offering a robust diagnostic performance with high token economy solution for resource-constrained clinical environments. 

4.2 Ablation Study 

We ablate MedPrune by sequentially removing its core components and report averaged results on Qwen3-VL (8B) and HuatuoGPT-Vision (7B). In Table 2, (1) removing NS or ES consistently causes the most substantial performance declines, confirm-ing that pruning irrelevant agents and redundant communication edges is indispensable for focusing computation on high-utility collaboration. It also directly addresses the inefficiencies of static and adaptive multi-agent topologies. (2) Removing the nuclear norm (w/o norm) causes moderate drops, confirming that low-rank priors aid compact topol-ogy. Ablating progressive pruning (w/o PP) results in smaller declines, confirming that a constant ratio yields strong baselines, while progressive pruning further refines MedPrune ’s performance edge. Ab-lating department selection (w/o DS) causes only minor performance drop, indicating fixed depart-ments suffice for promising results, while selection accelerates convergence via better initialization.

5 Conclusion 

We present MedPrune , a topology-efficient multi-agent collaborative framework for medical multimodal VQA. To reduce redundant communi-cation, we introduce heterogeneous node pruning that removes clinically irrelevant specialists, and heterogeneous edge pruning that retains diagnostically significant connections. Extensive experiments on medical VQA benchmarks show MedPrune consistently achieves superior diagnostic performance and token efficiency. Limitations 

Despite the promising results achieved by 

MedPrune , our work has several limitations that warrant further investigation. (1) The configuration of our sparsification framework involves several key hyperparameters, such as the node dropout rate 

α and the noise tolerance δ. Although we adopt a progressive pruning schedule and observe stable performance with the chosen settings, these values are not exhaustively optimized across all possible departmental compositions and imaging modalities. The optimal pruning aggressiveness may vary de-pending on the diagnostic question’s complexity and the number of collaborating specialists, sug-gesting a need for more adaptive pruning sched-ules in future work. (2) We introduce a simple and straightforward strategy in Appendix B.3 that selects candidate departments by relevance scor-ing using a frozen MLLM. This design provides a clean, lightweight baseline without introducing additional trainable components or complicating the pipeline. While effective, integrating depart-ment selection directly into reinforcement learning optimization could further streamline the workflow and reduce reliance on external ranking heuristics. (3) While our framework scales effectively with up to 15 agents across 3 departments, we do not eval-uate scenarios involving a much larger cohort of subspecialists. Future work should investigate the scalability of heterogeneous node and edge sparsi-fication in more granular clinical settings, where dozens of sub-specialty agents might be available, to stress-test the efficiency gains and ensure the pol-icy gradient optimization remains tractable under expanded combinatorial action spaces.

B Experimental Settings 

B.1 Data and Evaluation Metrics 

In Table 3, we summarize the training datasets’ statistics for training and testing. (1) MIMIC-CXR (Johnson et al., 2019) contains a large-scale chest radiograph dataset comprising 377,110 im-ages. Each image is associated with 14 pathology labels automatically extracted from corresponding free-text radiology reports (Xia et al., 2024a). (2) IU-Xray (Demner-Fushman et al., 2016) is a pub-licly available dataset comprising 3,996 radiology reports and 8,121 associated chest images. All data are rigorously de-identified with manual verifica-tion ensuring patient privacy (Xia et al., 2024a). (3) OmniMedVQA (Hu et al., 2024) is a large-scale medical VQA benchmark designed to eval-uate MLLMs. It comprises 118,010 real medical images and 127,995 QA pairs, covering 12 imag-ing modalities (e.g., MRI, CT and X-Ray) and over 20 anatomical regions. The dataset contains five question types, including disease diagnosis and modality recognition, all formatted as multi-choice questions. For evaluation, we report accuracy abbreviated as “Acc.” in classification tasks (i.e., IU-Xray and OmniMedVQA), where a response is correct only if it exactly matches the ground truth. For the gen-erative task in MIMIC-CXR, we compute “BLEU”, “ROUGE-L”, “BERT-F1”, and “METEOR” scores to comprehensively evaluate task performance. BLEU measures n-gram overlap between a model’s response and a reference. ROUGE-L evaluates re-call based on keyword or phrase overlap. BERT-F1 is the F1 score of BERTScore (Zhang et al., 2020), which harmonically averages precision and recall using pre-trained BERT embeddings to estimate se-mantic similarity. METEOR (Metric for Evaluation of Translation with Explicit ORdering) accounts for both fluency and semantics, offering a more flexible assessment (Hartsock and Rasool, 2024). 

B.2 Baseline 

(1) Training-free Methods. For standard prompt engineering (SP), the input image and question are formatted into a prompt with a MLLM and the model predicts a final response. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) en-hances medical VQA tasks by guiding the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps, improv-ing interpretability and enabling complex clinical reasoning. mRAG works can also enhance the medical reasoning ability of the model by retriev-ing external knowledge. MMed-RAG (Xia et al., 2025b) introduces a domain-aware retrieval mecha-nism, an adaptive retrieved contexts selection, and a provable RAG-based preference fine-tuning strat-egy. MIRA (Wang et al., 2025a) enhances the fac-tual accuracy in MLLMs via a calibrated adjust-ment module and a medical multimodal RAG for better knowledge integration. RULE (Xia et al., 2024b) mitigates factual errors by calibrating re-trieved context count and fine-tuning with prefer-ence data to balance knowledge utilization. (2) 

Training-intensive Methods. LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) performs parameter-efficient tuning by up-dating low-rank matrices while freezing pre-trained weights. QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023) extends it with 4-bit quantization, greatly reducing mem-ory usage while maintaining comparable model performance. Most competitive multi-agent baselines require updating full graph topology, leading to substantial train-ing costs. In contrast, MedPrune freezes the en-tire vision-language backbone and only optimizes lightweight soft adjacency matrices via policy gra-dient, significantly reducing training resource con-sumption.
